Do instruments that look alike also sound alike?

868 instruments from 7 museums, each with a photograph and a recording of that same object (recordings shared by several objects are left out). The photos were grouped blind by k-means on DINOv2 features, with no labels. Pick a photo group and listen: the recordings in it should sound like one family.

  • 0.80AUC that a shared photo group predicts a high audio similarity, with as many groups as the expert code has classes (17); 0.91 with 40 groups; chance 0.50
  • 0.68AMI between blind photo groups and blind sound groups of the same objects
  • 0.91the same AUC for a shared Hornbostel-Sachs code (17 classes), the expert reference

Photo groups, largest first (majority instrument name, number of objects)

Each card shows the object's instrument name from the MIMO vocabulary (or its museum title in grey when no name is recorded), its Hornbostel-Sachs class as a coloured tag, its museum, and the blind sound group the recording fell into (20 sound groups, k-means on CLAP audio features). A photo group whose cards share one sound group is one that sounds alike.

Excerpts are the loudest 3 seconds of each museum recording, mono, 16 kHz. Photos and recordings: MIMO member museums through Europeana, under each museum's own licence. Objects shown are the 10 nearest the centre of each photo group.
